# Cold review, six personas — 2026-08-06

Six independent agents evaluated the live site with no access to the repo,
strategy docs, or project context. Each wore one hat: an affiliate manager
receiving our application, a $600K buyer, a $2M comparison shopper mid-CRM
selection, an English teacher hunting AI prose, a consistency auditor, and a
25-year fundraising veteran reading the gaps. Run on Fable, 2026-08-06.

## Scoreboard

| Persona | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Affiliate manager | **Approve with conditions.** "Better than 90% of what crosses my desk" on disclosure; identity via W-9, fix the dates, publish survey method |
| Buyer ($600K) | **Partially trust.** Would shortlist from it, would not spend $9,800 on an unnamed byline |
| Comparison shopper ($2M) | **"Trust its prices, ignore its recommendations."** All 6 price checks verified against vendors |
| English teacher | **AI-drafted, human-edited, 85–90% confidence.** The tell is uniformity, not bad sentences |
| Auditor | **Minor drift, one material contradiction.** Every traced price consistent across ~40 pages |
| Fundraising expert | **Below $1M "the most honest software guide in the sector." Above $1M the advice thins exactly where the author's experience does** |

## The one finding four personas converged on

**The DonorDock pattern in aggregate.** Individually, every DonorDock claim
verified against the vendor. In aggregate: it is the Editor's Pick on the
homepage, the CRM hub, the best-of page, the churches page, three of four
stacks, and the top slot of nearly every alternatives page, always with a
reader deal attached — while its own caps (5 users then $40/mo each, 10K
emails/mo, 1K texts, 10 automations) are stated nowhere, on pages that
explicitly flag Bloomerang's "unlimited users." The shopper's phrasing:
"partially earned, structurally bought." The expert would contest the $5M+
pick in a board meeting. The disclosure claim "affiliate relationships never
decide a recommendation" survives on every individual page and does not
survive the pattern viewed in aggregate.

## What survived contact intact

- **Every price.** Three personas independently cross-checked ~12 pricing
  claims against vendor pages. Zero failures. The freshness stamps are real.
- **The /go/ architecture.** Active tracking code only where a relationship
  exists, plain URLs elsewhere. First thing the affiliate manager checked.
- **Per-page disclosure**, no fabricated testing claims, free tools
  recommended by name where they fit (Zeffy, LGL, Candid by library card).
- **The billing-axis analysis** (what re-prices your contract: records vs
  revenue vs flat) — "a tiebreaker lens Capterra and G2 don't offer."
- **The grants and prospect-research content** read as unmistakably
  first-hand to the expert. The consulting page read as the only fully
  human-voiced page to the teacher.

## Fix list B — strategic, operator's call

- **The DonorDock aggregate pattern.** Options: dial the placement back at
  the top tiers where it's least defensible (the expert's board-meeting
  objection), or defend it explicitly on-page. Doing nothing leaves the
  pattern for anyone who looks — and three different personas looked.
- **Rebuild the $5M+ stack as a real institutional tier** (RE NXT, Salesforce
  with named admin costs, Virtuous) with TCO math, reconciled against the
  benchmark. The raw material already exists on our own platform pages.
- **The digital annual-fund layer** — the expert's read of our blind spot:
  email platforms, matching gifts (zero mentions sitewide), peer-to-peer
  (the Classy page calls it "the real loss" then never fills it),
  text-to-give. Even a single honest hub of "what not to buy until $X."
- **/alternatives/etapestry** — "the highest-intent missing page on the
  site, squarely inside the author's demonstrated competence."
- **Voice.** The teacher's prescription matches the operator-input slots
  already in the drafts: one dated, unflattering, specific memory per money
  page; ration "rather than" and the closing epigrams; let compare pages
  read cheaper than essays. The uniform polish is itself the tell.

## Where personas disagreed

- The buyer *trusted* the site because of the break-even math and free picks;
  the shopper said ignore the recommendations. Same evidence, different
  stakes: at $600K the site's picks are defensible, at $2M they weren't
  built for the question.
- The teacher wants rougher, more human prose; the affiliate manager praised
  the consistency and hygiene. Both are right: keep the consistency in the
  data, roughen the voice in the judgment.
