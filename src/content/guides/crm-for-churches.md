The honest answer to "best donor management software for churches" is that most churches are asking the wrong question. The software that runs a congregation is church management software — attendance, groups, pastoral care, check-in. A donor CRM does none of that, and no amount of configuration will make it.

## When a church actually needs a donor CRM

Four situations, and they have something in common: donors who aren't members.

**A capital campaign.** Building funds bring in gifts from alumni families, former members who moved away, local businesses and foundations. None of those people are in your ChMS, and the campaign needs pledge schedules, ask amounts and a real solicitation history.

**An endowment or planned giving program.** These run on relationships tracked over decades, with contact reports and cultivation stages a membership system has no place to put.

**A parochial school or preschool.** The annual fund has parents, grandparents and alumni families as constituent types, and the giving patterns look nothing like Sunday offerings.

**A separate foundation or 501(c)(3).** If the money is legally separate, the records usually should be too.

If none of those describe you, spend the money on your ChMS instead and skip this page.

## The two-system problem, honestly

Churches that need both end up maintaining two lists, and the lists diverge within a year. There's no clean fix, but there is a discipline that works: pick one system as the system of record for people, and let the other hold only what it uniquely needs.

In practice that usually means the ChMS owns the household record and the donor CRM owns the campaign. Reconcile quarterly, not annually — a year of drift is a data-cleaning project, a quarter is an afternoon.

## What to test before you buy

Ask for a January demo of the annual giving statement. Not a screenshot — a real one, generated from imported test data that includes a joint gift from a couple, a non-cash gift, and a dinner ticket where the giver received something in return. That last one is the quid-pro-quo disclosure, and it is where church giving statements most often go wrong.

Then test pledges. Enter a three-year pledge with monthly payments, skip a month, and see what the system does. Some produce a clean balance and a reminder. Some quietly stop tracking it.

## On the free options

Zeffy and Givebutter cost nothing because donors are asked to add a tip at checkout. That's a real trade-off in a church context and worth raising with your finance committee before it appears on the giving page — some congregations find it unremarkable, others object to a third party asking their members for money on the way to the offering. Neither reaction is wrong, but you want to know which one you'll get before the first Sunday.
