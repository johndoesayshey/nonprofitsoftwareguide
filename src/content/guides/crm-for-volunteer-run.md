All-volunteer organizations get advice written for staffed nonprofits, and it does not fit. The constraint here is not money and it is not features. It is continuity.

## Buy for the person who inherits it

Whoever chooses the system will hand it to someone else, probably within two years, quite possibly with no handover at all.

That single fact should decide the purchase. The best software for an all-volunteer group is not the most capable one; it is the one a new treasurer can open cold, understand in an afternoon, and use without asking anyone.

It is also why [Little Green Light](/platforms/littlegreenlight/), which is genuinely excellent value, carries a warning in this vertical. Its strength is configurability, and configurability means the system ends up shaped around one volunteer's mental model. Their successor inherits custom fields whose purpose nobody remembers. If you go that route, make documenting it part of the job.

## The failure that actually happens

It is almost never "we picked the wrong CRM." It is this:

The treasurer who set everything up has moved away. The account was registered to her personal Gmail. The subscription renews on a card belonging to a board chair who left last year. Nobody currently involved knows which vendor it is, and the donor list is inside it.

Three habits prevent all of that, and none of them cost anything:

**Register every account to an address the organization controls.** `treasurer@yourgroup.org`, never a personal Gmail. If you don't have a domain, a shared organizational mailbox will do.

**Keep a one-page account record** in the board's shared files, every service, who owns it, how it is billed, when it renews. Not in an inbox. Not in a personal folder.

**Make sure two current people can get into everything.** One is a single point of failure and that person will eventually move.

## Free is not a compromise at this size

Most of this site argues that free platforms have real trade-offs. At all-volunteer scale, those trade-offs mostly don't bite.

[Zeffy](/go/zeffy) is free at any volume including processing fees (a $100 donation deposits $100) and covers donations, ticketing, raffles and memberships. [Givebutter](/go/givebutter) is free with donor tips on and adds peer-to-peer.

The one thing to settle first: both are funded by asking your donors to add an optional tip at checkout. Some boards find that unremarkable; others object to a third party soliciting their supporters. Raise it before it appears on the giving page, not after someone notices.

## When a database starts to earn its price

Not at a donor count. At a behavior.

A spreadsheet holds 300 donors perfectly well. What it cannot do is tell you who gave last year and hasn't this year, hold what was said at the last conversation, or survive being handed between four people.

The moment to buy is when someone wants to make a *second* ask to last year's donors and can't work out who they were. That's a real capability, and it's what a database provides that a list does not. If nobody is going to make that ask, the database will not pay for itself no matter how cheap it is.
