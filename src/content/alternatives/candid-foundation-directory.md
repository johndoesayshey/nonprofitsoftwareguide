Foundation Directory is the most authoritative foundation dataset available, and the case for leaving it is almost never about data quality. It's about the fact that a reference work doesn't manage a grant calendar, and that the same reference work is free at the library.

## Check the free route first

Candid partner libraries (Funding Information Network locations) offer full on-site access to Foundation Directory at no charge. Hundreds of them, in every state. Not a trial, not a limited version: the database, at the desk.

For a shop doing a research push twice a year, this replaces the subscription outright. Block two afternoons before your fall application season, work through your candidate list, take notes, and go home. The cost is the drive and the inconvenience of not having it at 9pm on a Tuesday.

That inconvenience is the actual argument for subscribing, and it's a legitimate one, research that has to be scheduled tends not to happen. But it should be a deliberate purchase of convenience, not a default.

## What you're really missing

Most shops searching for an alternative don't have a data problem. They have a tracking problem: which applications are out, what's due when, which reports are overdue, who's writing what. Foundation Directory has never done that, and no version of it will.

That job is what [Instrumentl](/go/instrumentl) is priced for. At $2,148 a year for the entry tier it is expensive against the category, our 2026 survey put mean grant research spend at $250 a year under $250K raised, $746 at $250K–$1M, and $2,278 at $1M–$5M. Instrumentl is only inside the band at $1M and up. Below that it has to be justified by grants won, not by convenience.

The cheap version of the same fix: a shared spreadsheet with columns for funder, amount, submitted date, decision date, and report due date, reviewed at a standing monthly meeting. It works. It fails when more than two people touch it, or when nobody owns the review.

## Reading 990s either way

Whichever tool you use, the discipline matters more than the source. A foundation's stated priorities are marketing; its 990 giving history is behaviour. When the two disagree, the giving history is the truth, and applying against the stated priorities is how a strong proposal gets declined for reasons nobody ever explains to you.

Both Foundation Directory and Instrumentl put grant history in front of you. GrantStation is thinner here, if you go that route, pair it with the library for diligence on any funder you're seriously pursuing. That combination runs about $199 a year and is the most cost-effective grant research setup available to a small shop.
