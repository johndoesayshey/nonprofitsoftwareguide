Most shops don't leave DonorPerfect because it can't do the job. They leave because the invoice moves in the wrong direction: up with the record count, up with the feature tier, up with the support package. The alternative that fixes that is usually a system with published, flat, or small-step pricing.

## Sort the complaint before you shop

Three different problems get described as "we need to leave DonorPerfect," and they have three different answers.

**If the problem is cost trajectory**, you want flat-tier pricing with unlimited contacts. [DonorDock](/go/donordock) is the cleanest version of that structure — the bill does not move when the list does. Eleo is the cheaper answer if your list is not growing fast and you'd rather pay small published steps than a flat tier.

**If the problem is adoption**, price is not the lever. A cheaper system nobody logs into produces the same stale data at a lower price. Bloomerang costs more than what you're leaving in some configurations and is still the right call when the development director, the ED and a part-time gift processor all need to touch the record.

**If the problem is a specific missing feature**, get precise before you migrate. Memberships, event registration and volunteer tracking are the three that usually drive a search, and [Neon CRM](/platforms/neoncrm/) carries all three natively at a published add-on percentage.

## The number to compare

Our 2026 survey of 100 U.S. fundraising professionals put mean annual donor CRM spend at $716 under $250K raised, $2,726 at $250K–$1M, and $7,130 at $1M–$5M. Take your DonorPerfect renewal, add support and any module you pay for, and compare the total to the band for your size. If you're inside it, the migration is discretionary. If you're well above it, you have a finance-committee case that doesn't depend on anyone's opinion of the software.

## Before you export

Ask for a full data export while you are still a customer in good standing, and confirm what comes with it — gift records, pledge schedules, soft credits, custom fields and any linked constituent relationships. Pledge schedules and recurring-gift records are where DonorPerfect migrations most often lose fidelity, because every system models them slightly differently. Have the new vendor import your actual file, not a sample, before you sign anything.

Then run both systems for one full gift cycle. Reconcile the totals against your deposits. Close the old one only when the numbers match.
