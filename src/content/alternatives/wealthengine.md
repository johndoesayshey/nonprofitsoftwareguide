WealthEngine models wealth well and publishes nothing about what it costs. Both of those facts drive alternative searches — the first because wealth alone is a partial answer, and the second because a quote with no reference point is a bad negotiating position.

## The test before you shop anything

Ask who, by name, will make the visits, and whether that's on their calendar. If there's no answer, no screening product will pay for itself, and hand-researching your top 25 prospects is the cheaper experiment.

Our 2026 survey of 100 U.S. fundraising professionals found 95% of shops under $250K raised spend $0 on wealth screening — median $0, mean $45. At $250K–$1M the mean is $1,319 and the median $550, meaning half that segment still spends nothing. At $1M–$5M the mean is $4,135, and at $5M+ it's $17,320. The category effectively starts at $1M raised.

## Capacity is half the picture

This is the substantive reason to look elsewhere, and it's worth separating from the price complaint.

A wealth-indicator model tells you who has money — real estate, securities, business affiliations, giving to other organizations where it's public. It does not tell you who gives. A list ranked purely on capacity sends gift officers to wealthy people with no philanthropic history, and those visits convert badly.

Demonstrated giving is the better predictor, which is [DonorSearch](/go/donorsearch)'s explicit thesis. Kindsight sits between the two and shows its sourcing. Which weighting is right for you depends on your file: wealthy people who haven't given yet, or modest people who give every year without fail.

## Buy the screen, not the year

Pre-campaign feasibility is a project, not an ongoing need. DonorSearch's pay-per-record option — reported at roughly $1–$2 per record — screens a 5,000-record file once with no subscription attached. For a one-time capacity picture that is the whole job, at a fraction of any annual contract plus implementation fee.

The consultant route does the same thing and adds the part no software does: turning scores into a tiered portfolio with suggested ask amounts. That typically runs around $4,000–$5,000 as a project. For a first screen, the interpretation is worth more than the data — [our consulting page](/consulting/) covers how those engagements work.

## Run a real comparison

Build a test file of 50–100 constituents whose capacity you can independently verify — board members, past major donors, people whose giving elsewhere is public. Ask every vendor to screen it during the sales process. Most will.

Check who they flagged, who they missed, and how many high scores you know are wrong. The vendor whose output best matches what you already know is the one your team will trust on the names you don't.

Then tell each vendor you're running the same file elsewhere. Every product in this category is quoted per organization, which means the price reflects the negotiation — and a competing bid is the only real leverage you have. It works better before you sign than at renewal.
