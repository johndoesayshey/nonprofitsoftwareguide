Little Green Light is the best value in donor CRM software, and that is not in dispute. Shops leave it for one reason far more often than any other: the people who need to use it, don't.

## The adoption test

Answer this honestly. In the last month, did anyone besides the primary database person log in and do something useful — look up a donor before a meeting, enter a gift, pull a list?

If yes, LGL is working and switching is a waste of a quarter. If no, you have a data-quality problem that will get worse, and it is not solved by more training. It is solved by software people will open. That is what [Bloomerang](/go/bloomerang) sells, and it is the honest reason to pay more.

## What the alternatives actually change

**Nothing here is more configurable than LGL.** Custom fields, custom categories, flexible reporting — LGL gives you more of that per dollar than Bloomerang, DonorDock, Eleo or Neon. If you move, you will hit a wall somewhere that LGL would have let you build around. Know that going in.

**What you gain is surfacing.** DonorDock's ActionBoard and Bloomerang's retention dashboard both do the same structural thing: they decide what to show you. LGL waits to be asked. For a solo admin who knows their file, waiting is fine. For a team, it means the follow-up doesn't happen.

**The cost delta is small in absolute terms.** Our 2026 survey of 100 U.S. fundraising professionals put mean annual donor CRM spend at $716 under $250K raised and $2,726 at $250K–$1M. LGL at 2,500 constituents is $540 a year. Bloomerang at Standard is around $1,500. That difference is a single mid-size gift. If the migration produces even a modest lift in retention, it pays for itself — and if nothing changes about who logs in, it won't.

## Migration notes

LGL exports cleanly, which makes this one of the easier moves in the category. Pull constituents, gifts, pledges, custom fields and any relationship links. The things to verify on the far side are soft credits and household records — every system models joint gifts differently, and that's where a decade of major-gift history gets flattened into individual records.

If you're moving primarily for memberships or events, check whether the second system you're already running can simply become the system of record for that slice. Two clean systems with a defined boundary beat one system doing a job it wasn't built for.
