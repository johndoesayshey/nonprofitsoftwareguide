Most shops do not leave eTapestry because it stopped working. They leave because they cannot answer a board member who asks what the database will cost in three years, and the systems they are comparing against can answer that in one screenshot.

## What actually pushes shops out

The renewal conversation is the trigger almost every time. eTapestry is quoted, not listed, so the annual number arrives without context: no published tier you can point to, no way to check it against what a comparable organization pays. For a two-person shop that reviews vendors once a year at most, the practical result is a price that only ever moves in one direction.

The second push is growth. eTapestry is sold as the small-team product in a catalog built around institutions, and the escalation path runs toward Raiser's Edge NXT. That is a reasonable destination for a university or a hospital foundation. For a $700,000 shop that added 4,000 names after a good year, it is a step into a different cost bracket and a system that expects a database administrator. Plenty of organizations reach that fork and decide the honest move is out of the catalog, not up it.

## The number you cannot look up

This is the part worth being concrete about. Every alternative recommended here publishes its full price ladder. Little Green Light runs $45/mo at 2,500 constituents to about $135/mo at 50,000. Eleo starts at $39/mo and prints each step to $199/mo at 20,000 records. DonorDock is a single $6,000 a year with unlimited contacts. Bloomerang's CRM entry is $1,500 a year with unlimited users.

You can put any of those in a budget line without talking to anyone. Whatever else changes in a migration, that is the change a finance committee understands immediately, and it is the strongest argument you have if you need one.

Our 2026 survey of 100 U.S. fundraising professionals put mean annual donor CRM spend at $716 for shops under $250K raised, $2,726 at $250K to $1M, and $7,130 at $1M to $5M. Take your eTapestry renewal, add anything you pay separately, and compare the total with the band for your size. Sitting inside it means the migration is discretionary. Sitting well above it means you have a case that does not rest on anyone's opinion of the software.

## Before you export

Ask for a full export while you are still a customer in good standing, and confirm what comes with it: gift records, pledge schedules, soft credits, custom fields, attributes and any linked constituent relationships. Pledges and recurring gifts are where these migrations lose fidelity, because every system models them a little differently and the differences only surface once the data has landed.

Then have the new vendor import your actual file, not a sample. A demo built on clean sample data tells you nothing about what your fifteen years of accumulated custom fields will do to it. Run both systems through one full gift cycle, reconcile the totals against your bank deposits, and close the old account only when the two agree.

One more thing worth doing first: get the renewal quote in writing before you start shopping. It is the only number in this whole exercise you cannot look up, and you will want it in front of you when you compare.
