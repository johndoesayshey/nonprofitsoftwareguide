Before comparing anything, open your Givebutter dashboard and find your real tip rate, what share of donors covered the platform's cut. That single number tells you whether you have a cost problem at all.

If most donors are tipping, your effective platform cost is close to zero and every alternative here is more expensive. If your tip rate is low, or you've already turned tips off and are paying about 3%, then you're paying a competitive-market rate for a platform positioned as free, and it's worth running the math below.

## The break-even, with your numbers

Take last year's online giving total.

- **At roughly 3%** (tips off), $50,000 online costs about $1,500 a year. $150,000 costs about $4,500.
- **[4aGoodCause](/go/4agoodcause) at $1,188/yr flat** beats a 3% fee from roughly $40,000 of online giving to its $50K cap, and from about $100,000 the flat tiers (Growing to $250K, then Scale) win at every volume. That is the entire argument for a subscription: the fee stops scaling with your success.
- **[Donorbox](/go/donorbox) at roughly 2.95%** is a marginal improvement on 3% at low volume; the real case for it is the Pro tier at about $150/mo plus roughly 1.75%, which crosses over around $150,000 online.
- **[Zeffy](/go/zeffy)** is $0 at any volume, including processing fees, on the same tip-funded model you already have.

Processing fees apply on top of the platform fee everywhere except Zeffy. Compare total effective rate, not headline platform rate.

## The question underneath the search

A large share of "Givebutter alternatives" searches aren't really about fees. They're from shops that have outgrown having no donor database.

Givebutter holds transactions and contact records. It does not hold that a gift from a donor-advised fund should soft-credit the couple who recommended it, that two donors are one household, or that a lapsed $500 donor from 2023 is worth a call. Those are CRM jobs. If that's your actual gap, switching checkout platforms will not fix it; you need a [donor CRM](/donor-crm/) behind whatever checkout you keep, and keeping Givebutter for the free ticketing is often the right call.

## If you do switch

Recurring donors are the cost of any processor change. Cards on file don't transfer between platforms, so monthly donors have to re-enroll. Assume attrition, run a real re-enrollment campaign with a deadline, and call anyone giving more than $50 a month personally. Do it in a quiet month, never during a campaign or in December.
