Donorbox is priced as a percentage, which is the right structure at low volume and the wrong one once you grow. Most alternative searches come from one of those two ends — a shop too small to be paying anything, or a shop big enough that the percentage has become a real line item.

## Find which end you're on

Multiply last year's online giving by 2.95%. That's your platform fee, excluding card processing.

**Under about $40,000 online?** Your fee is roughly $1,200 or less. [Zeffy](/go/zeffy) at $0 or [Givebutter](/go/givebutter) at 0% with tips on will both beat it, and no flat subscription will. The tradeoff is real but modest: both are funded by an optional donor tip, so your net per gift depends on how many donors leave it on.

**Between roughly $40,000 and $150,000?** This is the band where a flat subscription wins. [4aGoodCause](/go/4agoodcause) at $1,188/yr takes no percentage at all, so at $100,000 online you pay $1,188 instead of about $2,950. Donorbox Pro doesn't help here — at about $150/mo plus roughly 1.75%, it costs more than Standard until you're past roughly $150k.

**Past $150,000?** Compare Donorbox Pro (about $1,800/yr plus roughly 1.75%) against 4aGoodCause Growth ($2,988/yr, no percentage). At $200,000 online, Pro runs about $5,300 and Growth about $2,988. The flat subscription is still ahead, and it stays ahead.

Card processing fees apply on top of all of these, typically around 2.2–3% plus a fixed amount per transaction. Zeffy is the exception.

## What the percentage is actually buying

Don't switch on the fee alone. Donorbox's recurring-gift management is the best thing about it, and it produces revenue: a donor portal that lets people update their own card, automatic retries and dunning on failed payments, and prompts to upgrade a monthly gift. Card failure alone silently kills a meaningful share of monthly donors at platforms that don't chase them.

Work out what share of your online revenue is recurring. If it's substantial, price the retention tooling on any alternative before you move — saving $1,500 in fees while losing $4,000 in lapsed monthly gifts is a bad trade that shows up two quarters later.

## The migration cost nobody quotes

Recurring donors have to re-enroll. Cards on file stay with the payment processor and don't transfer between platforms. Ask in writing whether token migration is possible; assume it isn't. Then run a real re-enrollment campaign with a deadline, call your larger monthly donors personally, and do the whole thing in a quiet month.
