Crossing $250k is where the first paid tools earn their keep. You have outgrown the free stack in two places, donor records and grant prospecting, and this stack spends on exactly those. Budget about $3,700 a year in subscriptions, plus processing fees and whatever your event quote comes back at. Wealth screening stays off the software list on purpose.

## Why these picks

**CRM: DonorDock (~$98/mo, unlimited contacts).** The first real database, and the one built for a team that does not have a database person. [DonorDock's](/go/donordock) ActionBoard turns donor records into a daily list of who to thank, call and follow up with, which is the part small teams actually drop. Unlimited contacts on every tier means a growing list never surprises you with a bigger bill, and the 90-day money-back guarantee makes it the lowest-risk trial in the category. Readers get 10% off through this site. Worth comparing against [Little Green Light](/go/littlegreenlight) if price is the binding constraint, and [Bloomerang](/go/bloomerang) if you want deeper retention reporting, though both bill by record count.

**Grant research: Instrumentl ($179/mo).** The biggest line and the easiest to test. [Instrumentl](/go/instrumentl) earns its price when prospecting is a recurring monthly job: the matching finds funders you would not, and the pipeline keeps deadlines from slipping between two people. Load your active applications into the 14-day trial and see what the matching surfaces before you commit.

**Money in: Donorbox (free to start, ~2.95% platform fee).** At this size recurring giving becomes a program, and that is what [Donorbox](/go/donorbox) is good at: donor portals, failed-card recovery, and campaign pages that do not look homemade. Nothing to pay until money moves.

Check your own break-even before you settle, because it moves. A percentage costs less than a flat subscription below roughly $40,000 of online giving and more above it. Past that point [4aGoodCause](/go/4agoodcause) at $1,188 a year takes no percentage at all and stops scaling with your success — which is the upgrade to make when online giving outgrows the fee.

Do one piece of arithmetic before you settle, though. A percentage fee is a tax on getting better at this, and at 2.95% you cross $1,188 a year in platform fees at about $40,000 of online giving. Past that, [4aGoodCause](/go/4agoodcause) at a flat $99 a month costs less, and the gap widens every year the program grows. Multiply last year's online total by 0.0295. If the answer is over about $1,200, price both. If keeping every dollar matters more than either, [Zeffy](/go/zeffy) is still free.

**Operations: monday.com (~$216-$432/yr).** Same role as before, more seats. Grant deliverables, event run-of-show, program dashboards. Apply to the nonprofit program before paying list price.

**Wealth screening: not software.** At this size the recommendation is a [consultant-run screen](/consulting/) rather than a subscription, for the reasons set out below the ledger.

**Events: Greater Giving (quoted).** Only if your gala is genuinely an auction rather than a dinner with a raffle. [Greater Giving](/go/greatergiving) sells the whole night as a package, mobile bidding and check-in and payment hardware together, which is the difference from a general fundraising platform when a hundred people are bidding at once. Get the quote early in your event cycle, and ask what is hardware versus rental. If the event is modest, run it on your registration forms for another year.

## When you've outgrown this stack

Move up to the [Established stack](/stacks/1m-5m/) when major-gift work starts in earnest and screening needs to run more than once, when your CRM's reporting stops answering board questions, or when grant volume needs more seats and more active projects at once.
