Crossing $250k is where the first paid tools earn their keep, and the range is honest about the choice: the core stack (CRM and giving platform on one bill, plus forms) runs about $3,000 a year, and adding both optional software lines (grant research, the event package) takes it to roughly $13,300. Our benchmark of 100 fundraisers puts mean software spend at this size at $10,197, so this stack brackets what your peers already spend. Wealth screening stays off the software list on purpose.

## Why these picks

**Donor CRM + donation processing: one line, two options.** At this size the honest move is to stop buying the database and the giving pages separately.

The default is [4aGoodCause](/go/4agoodcause) on the Growing plan at $2,988 a year: giving pages that take no percentage of donations (our editor's choice for processing) with a built-in donor CRM riding along at no extra line item. Profiles, giving history, notes, tags, custom fields and acknowledgement letters, verified on their own feature page. It has no built-in email marketing; the Mailchimp integration carries that. Growing covers up to $250,000 of online revenue a year, which is the figure to check rather than your total raised: a $600,000 shop taking $120,000 online sits comfortably inside it. A smaller online program can start on Grassroots ($99/mo, up to $50,000) and upgrade at renewal when it grows; donations never stop either way. Above $250,000 online, Scale is $429/mo ($5,148/yr) with no revenue ceiling.

If you feel like you need a more robust CRM, go with [DonorDock](/go/donordock): one plan at $500 a month billed annually with every feature in it, email marketing included, unlimited contacts, and the ActionBoard turning the database into a daily follow-up list. At $6,000 a year it is priced as a consolidation of your CRM, email tool and giving pages together, so judge it against that whole bill, and remember onboarding is a separate $3,800 one-time line.

Two cheaper paths still exist. Under about $40,000 of online giving a year, a percentage platform like [Donorbox](/go/donorbox) costs less than any flat subscription; multiply last year's online total by 0.0295 and compare it with the tier that covers your volume. And if keeping every dollar matters most, [Zeffy](/go/zeffy) is still free. Both hold donor records of their own; what you give up is the deeper CRM layer.

**Grant research: Instrumentl ($179/mo), optional until grants are a program.** The biggest line and the easiest to test. [Instrumentl](/go/instrumentl) earns its price when prospecting is a recurring monthly job: the matching finds funders you would not, and the pipeline keeps deadlines from slipping between two people. Load your active applications into the 14-day trial and see what the matching surfaces before you commit.

**Forms and operations: Jotform ($0 to about $17/mo).** Same tool as the free stack, doing more work: event registration, volunteer intake, program applications, and any form that needs to take a payment. The nonprofit discount halves the paid tier when the free plan's limits arrive.

**Wealth screening: not software.** At this size the recommendation is a [consultant-run screen](/consulting/) instead of a subscription, for the reasons set out below the ledger.

**Events: Greater Giving (~$3,000-$8,000, quoted).** Optional for most nonprofits at this size, the benchmark shows most of your peers spending nothing here. It earns the line only if your gala is genuinely an auction, not a dinner with a raffle: [Greater Giving](/go/greatergiving) sells the whole night as a package, mobile bidding and check-in and payment hardware together. Get the quote early in your event cycle, and ask what is hardware versus rental. If the event is modest, run it on your registration forms for another year.

## When you've outgrown this stack

Move up to the [Established stack](/stacks/1m-5m/) when major-gift work starts in earnest and screening needs to run more than once, when your CRM's reporting stops answering board questions, or when grant volume needs more seats and more active projects at once.
