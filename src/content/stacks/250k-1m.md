Crossing $250k is where the first paid tools earn their keep. You've outgrown the all-free stack in exactly two places — donor records and grant prospecting — and this stack spends on precisely those: about **$2,700 a year** for a real CRM and a real grant pipeline, with processing kept free. Each line maps to hours a 1–3 person team stops losing.

## Why these picks

**CRM — Little Green Light ($45/mo, up to 2,500 constituents).** The perennial small-shop value pick: flexible fields, real reporting, mailing tools, no contracts, and unlimited users, at roughly a third of what switchers report paying for bigger-name systems. The interface is dated — that's the tradeoff for the price — but the capability per dollar is unmatched at this size. One discipline before you migrate: clean the list first, and check that gift history and soft credits import cleanly. Migration is where donor data dies, and export fidelity is the thing to verify while you can still walk away. If your team needs the software to be inviting before anyone opens it, [Bloomerang](/platforms/bloomerang/) at $79/month is the friendlier runner-up; if you want the database to run your daily follow-up list, look at [DonorDock](/platforms/donordock/).

**Grant research — Instrumentl ($179/mo).** The biggest line, and the most debatable one — so here's the honest test. Plenty of experienced grant writers pay nothing: Grants.gov alerts, funder newsletters, 990s on ProPublica, and library-day access to Candid. If that describes your volume, keep the free workflow and pocket $2,148. Instrumentl earns its price when prospecting is a recurring monthly job and deadlines live across two people's heads — the matching finds funders you wouldn't, and the pipeline tracker is what actually protects revenue. A middle path if the budget is tight: [GrantStation via TechSoup](/platforms/grantstation/) at ~$199/yr is a tenth the cost for a searchable database, minus the workflow.

**Processing — Zeffy ($0).** Nothing at this size justifies giving up a percentage of every gift. Revisit only when a specific integration or recurring-giving need forces it.

## What to cut first when the budget gets cut

Cut Instrumentl before the CRM. The database holds your donor history — losing momentum there sets you back years, and there's no free substitute once you depend on it. Grant research can revert to the free workflow for a season without destroying the program; it costs hours, not history. The CRM is the load-bearing wall; the research tool is leverage, and leverage is what you trim when protecting the core.

## When you've outgrown this stack

Move up to the [Established stack](/stacks/1m-5m/) when: major-gift work starts in earnest and you need wealth screening to point limited cultivation time; the board is asking retention and pipeline questions LGL's reporting makes you assemble by hand; or grant volume needs more than one seat and more than five active projects. That's when the stack jumps from ~$2,700 to five figures — and starts paying for itself in bigger ways.
