At $1M–$5M the stack changes shape: wealth screening enters, because you now have major-gift work worth prioritizing, and the CRM becomes the system the whole team lives in. Budget **$11,000–$14,000 a year** all-in. This is the tier where skipping prospect research actively costs you — and where every line item should map to a person whose time it protects.

## Why these picks

**CRM — Bloomerang (~$125/mo at typical database sizes).** At this size the whole team touches the database, so ease of use stops being cosmetic — the CRM staff actually update beats the more powerful one they don't. Bloomerang's retention dashboards put lapsing donors in front of whoever logs in, and unlimited seats mean growth doesn't change the bill. The known ceiling: advanced automation and reporting run thinner than power users want. If your board reporting outgrows it, [Neon](/platforms/neoncrm/) adds breadth (memberships, events) at the cost of complexity — and check its data exports during the trial, because practitioner forums carry real scar tissue about exit costs.

**Prospect research — DonorSearch (quoted; ~$4,000–$6,000/yr reported).** The new line, and the one with a condition attached: screening pays off only when a named person works the results into portfolios and visits. DonorSearch leads with demonstrated philanthropy — actual gifts to other organizations — which veteran researchers weight over raw wealth markers, because giving behavior predicts giving. Get the quote in writing, make [Kindsight](/platforms/kindsight/) quote the same seats and volume, and confirm the CRM integration before signing.

**Grant research — Instrumentl Standard ($299/mo).** Grants are a program now: multiple staff in the pipeline, ten or more active applications, renewals stacked on new submissions. The shared deadline pipeline is what keeps a team-scale calendar from slipping — this is exactly the volume where the subscription stops being debatable.

**Processing — Donorbox Pro ($150/mo).** At this volume, recurring-gift management earns its keep: failed-card recovery and donor portals protect monthly revenue you already earned. Pro's lower platform fee (~1.75% vs ~2.95%) roughly pays for the subscription at $150k+ of online giving — run your own number.

## What to cut first when the budget gets cut

Cut prospect research first. It's the newest line and the most deferrable — run last year's screen another cycle, or hand-research your top 50. Then downgrade Donorbox Pro to the free tier (the fee difference is real but survivable). Protect the CRM and grant research last: one holds your history, the other funds the shortfall you're trying to close.

## When you've outgrown this stack

Move up to the [Institutional stack](/stacks/5m-plus/) when screening needs to run continuously instead of annually, your signature event outgrows free tooling, or the CRM needs real permissions and membership structure. That's the jump from five figures to the low twenties.
