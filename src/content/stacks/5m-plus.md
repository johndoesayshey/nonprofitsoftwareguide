The question is no longer "which tools" but "which tools integrate and who owns each." Every line here supports a role — a researcher, a gift officer, an events lead — and the cost is small against the salaries it makes productive.

## What to cut first when the budget gets cut

Cut the newest event or processing add-on before touching the CRM or screening — those are load-bearing at this scale. But the more honest answer at $5M+ is that software is rarely where the cut should land: these line items are cheap against staff time, and trimming the tool that makes a $90k gift officer productive is a false economy. Cut scope or contract terms, not the core stack.

## When you've outgrown this stack

Past this point the decisions get bespoke — enterprise CRM builds, custom data warehouses, integration engineering. The stack stops being a shopping list and becomes an architecture question for a systems hire, not a guide page.
