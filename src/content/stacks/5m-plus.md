At $5M+ the stack is a full operation: continuous wealth screening, a system-of-record CRM, team-scale grant research, high-volume processing, and dedicated event software. Budget roughly **$18,000–$28,000 a year**. The question is no longer "which tools" but "which tools integrate and who owns each" — every line here supports a role, and the cost is small against the salaries it makes productive.

## Why these picks

**CRM — Neon (revenue-scaled, negotiate).** At this size you need permissions, membership handling, and event integration in the database of record, and Neon's breadth covers it — with two provisos from the people who've lived it. First, model the real quote: add-on modules run +10–20% of the subscription. Second, test the exports during the trial (full constituent-and-gift-history pull, checked against source), because your CRM choice is also your exit-cost choice. The alternative at this scale is a Salesforce build — and the standing warning there is "free like a puppy": the donated licenses are the cheap part, and without a budgeted admin or consultant you inherit an unmaintainable custom mess. Choose Salesforce only with a named owner and implementation budget.

**Prospect research — Kindsight (~$5,350/yr Professional, reported).** Screening runs continuously now — new donors screened as they arrive, portfolios refreshed on a schedule, a researcher or MG lead who owns the output. Kindsight's transparent, sourced scoring is why it tops category satisfaction ratings; the discipline that makes it pay is the same as ever, capacity is not inclination, and visits are what convert scores into gifts.

**Grant research — Instrumentl Pro ($499/mo).** Twenty-plus active projects across a team is Pro-tier volume. At institutional scale, pair it with desk access to [Candid](/platforms/candid/) when a single funder deserves a deep workup.

**Processing — Donorbox Pro, Events — OneCause.** Pro-tier processing protects a large recurring file. The event line is the honest judgment call: OneCause's package-plus-5% is real money, and it buys a smooth six-figure night — mobile bidding, seating, check-in — that a free tool would improvise. If your gala is under ~$100k, [Givebutter](/platforms/givebutter/) does it free; size the tool to the event.

## What to cut first when the budget gets cut

Cut the event platform first (run the gala on Givebutter for a year), then drop screening from continuous to annual. But the more honest answer at $5M+: software is rarely where the cut belongs. These lines total under half a percent of revenue, and trimming the tool that makes a $90k gift officer productive is a false economy. Cut scope and contract terms, not the core stack.

## When you've outgrown this stack

Past this point the decisions go bespoke — enterprise CRM builds, data warehouses, integration engineering. The stack stops being a shopping list and becomes an architecture question for a systems hire.
