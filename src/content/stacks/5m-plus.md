At $5M+ the stack is a full operation: continuous wealth screening, a system-of-record CRM, team-scale grant research, high-volume processing, and dedicated event software. Budget roughly $18,000-$28,000 a year. Every line supports a role, and the cost is small against the salaries it makes productive.

## Why these picks

**CRM: Neon CRM (revenue-scaled).** At this size you need permissions, membership handling, and event integration in the database of record, and [Neon's](/go/neoncrm) breadth covers it. Take the demo with your real workflows: membership renewals, event registration, and the board reports you run quarterly. Price the add-on modules you'll actually use, and run a full test export during the trial, standard diligence with any system at this scale. The alternative is a Salesforce build, which can be excellent when a named admin and an implementation budget come with it; without those, teams inherit more system than they can maintain.

**Prospect research: Kindsight (~$5,350/yr Professional, reported).** Screening runs continuously now: new donors screened as they arrive, portfolios refreshed on a schedule, a researcher who owns the output. [Kindsight's](/go/kindsight) transparent scoring is why it tops category satisfaction ratings. The discipline that makes it pay is unchanged: capacity is not inclination, and visits convert scores into gifts.

**Grant research: Instrumentl Pro ($499/mo).** Twenty-plus active projects across a team is Pro-tier volume. [Instrumentl](/go/instrumentl) manages the pipeline; pair it with desk access to [Candid](/go/candid) when a single funder deserves a deep workup.

**Processing and events.** [Donorbox](/go/donorbox) Pro protects a large recurring file with failed-card recovery and donor portals. For the signature event, [OneCause](/go/onecause) turns a six-figure gala into a managed production, from mobile bidding to checkout. If your gala is under about $100k, [Givebutter](/go/givebutter) runs it free; size the tool to the event.

## What to cut first when the budget gets cut

Cut the event platform first (run the gala on Givebutter for a year), then drop screening from continuous to annual. Past that, look hard before cutting software at all: these lines total under half a percent of revenue, and trimming the tool that makes a $90k gift officer productive is a false economy.

## When you've outgrown this stack

Past this point the decisions go bespoke: enterprise CRM builds, data warehouses, integration engineering. The stack stops being a shopping list and becomes an architecture question for a systems hire.
