Paid grant research software is worth it when prospecting is a recurring monthly job. A small shop submitting a handful of applications a year can run on free sources: ProPublica 990s, on-site Candid access at a partner library, and funder newsletters. Once research becomes routine monthly work, a paid tool earns its cost in hours.

## The free path, and where it works

[Candid](/go/candid)'s Foundation Directory carries the deepest 990 and grant-history data in the field, and it is free to use on-site at hundreds of partner libraries. ProPublica's Nonprofit Explorer posts 990s free from any desk. Add a few funder and sector newsletters and you have a complete research setup at zero dollars.

The free path suits low volume. If you apply to a handful of funders a year and most are renewals, the library visit and the 990 reading fit inside the job.

## Where free breaks down

Free research costs hours. The library copy of Candid stays at the library. The 990s run a year or more behind. Nobody alerts you when a new funder enters your field or an old one changes direction. At low volume that is a manageable tax. At monthly volume it becomes the largest hidden line in your grant program, paid in the researcher's time.

## What paid buys, at three price points

[GrantStation](/go/grantstation) is the entry point: about $199 a year through TechSoup, with promotions near $150, for a searchable database plus weekly alerts. You still filter and track by hand. [Candid](/go/candid) Essential at about $449 a year moves the directory from the library to your desk. [Instrumentl](/go/instrumentl) starts at $179 a month billed annually, about $2,148 a year, and adds the two things the others lack: matching against your organization's profile and a deadline pipeline. [Our Instrumentl review](/platforms/instrumentl/) covers the tiers in detail.

## The hours framing

Skip abstract ROI math and run one simple estimate. Count the hours you spent last month on funder research and deadline tracking, and price them at what your time costs the organization. Then hold each tool against that number. A $199 database earns its keep if it saves a few hours a year. A $2,148 platform needs to save real time every month, which it can only do if research happens every month.

For most shops the answer tracks volume. Occasional applicants keep the free path or add GrantStation. Monthly prospectors come out ahead on the bigger tool, because the hours it returns go back into writing and stewardship, the two activities that actually win grants.

## Try before deciding

Instrumentl's 14-day free trial settles the question with your own data: load your profile and see whether two weeks of matches beat your last quarter of manual searching. GrantStation's price makes it a low-risk annual experiment. Either test costs almost nothing next to a single funded proposal.
