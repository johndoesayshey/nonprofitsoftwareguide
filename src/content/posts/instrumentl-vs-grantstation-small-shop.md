For a one-person shop, [GrantStation](/go/grantstation) at about $199 a year is the budget pick and [Instrumentl](/go/instrumentl) at $179 a month billed annually is the workflow pick. The deciding variable is application volume: a few proposals a year favors GrantStation; steady monthly prospecting with several deadlines in flight favors Instrumentl.

## What each one is

[GrantStation](/go/grantstation) is a searchable funder database with weekly alert emails. You run the searches, judge fit, and track deadlines yourself. Through TechSoup it costs about $199 a year, with promotions that dip near $150. The weekly alerts are a real asset for a solo fundraiser: new opportunities arrive without you hunting for them. There are no pipeline tools, so your spreadsheet stays the system of record.

[Instrumentl](/go/instrumentl) is a matching engine plus a grant pipeline. It suggests funders against your organization's profile, shows 990 data inline on each profile, and tracks every application from research through report. Plans start at $179 a month billed annually, about $2,148 a year, covering roughly 5 active projects. Standard is $299 a month for around 10, and Pro is $499 for around 20.

## What the price gap buys

Roughly a tenfold price difference buys time. With GrantStation you do the filtering, the 990 lookups, and the deadline tracking by hand. Instrumentl does the first pass of all three: matches arrive scored against your profile, the funder's giving history sits on the same screen, and deadlines live in a tracker instead of your head.

For a solo fundraiser, hours are the binding constraint. The grant program's ceiling is set by how many quality applications one person can research, write, and steward. If a tool returns even a few hours a month to writing and stewardship, that is the case for it. If research is an occasional task at your volume, the cheaper database plus your own diligence covers the job well.

## A decision rule by volume

If grants are a season for you (a handful of applications a year, most of them renewals to funders you know), buy GrantStation, keep your spreadsheet, and bank the difference. If grants are a program (new prospects every month, several open deadlines at once, report dates stacking up behind the awards), the pipeline is the feature you are actually buying, and Instrumentl is the one that has it.

One more consideration: neither tool writes the proposal. Both end at the point where your judgment and your program knowledge take over, so the comparison is only about how fast each gets you to a vetted, prioritized list of funders worth writing for.

## The cheap way to decide

Instrumentl's 14-day free trial is the lowest-cost way to settle this comparison. Load your real profile, let the matching run, and compare its suggestions against what your own searching surfaced last quarter. If the matches beat your list, the subscription has made its case; if they do not, GrantStation at $199 was the right answer and you found out for free.
