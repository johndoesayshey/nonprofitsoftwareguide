Little Green Light wins on price: $45/mo up to 2,500 constituents against Bloomerang's $79/mo start for roughly 1,000 contacts. Bloomerang wins on ease of use and retention dashboards. The decision comes down to who updates the database. A detail-oriented admin thrives in LGL; everyone else does better in Bloomerang.

## Where LGL wins

[Little Green Light](/go/littlegreenlight) is one of the best values in donor software. Seven pricing tiers run from $45/mo at 2,500 constituents to $135/mo at 50,000, then $15/mo per additional 10,000 records. No contracts, no setup fees, and 10% off if you prepay annually. A shop can grow from 2,000 donors to 20,000 without ever renegotiating anything.

It is also flexible. Custom fields, custom categories, and configurable everything. The tradeoff is a dated interface. LGL rewards the user who enjoys building things and punishes nobody, but it never holds your hand.

## Where Bloomerang wins

[Bloomerang](/go/bloomerang) starts around $79/mo for about 1,000 contacts, with the Standard tier around $125/mo, priced by contact-record count with unlimited users. It costs more than LGL at comparable sizes, and what the difference buys is adoption.

The interface is clean enough that a board member or program staffer can look up a donor without training. The retention dashboards are the signature feature: they show, on the home screen, whether donors are coming back, which is the number that actually moves annual fund revenue. Bloomerang is lighter on advanced automation, but shops at this size rarely miss it.

## The decision rule: who updates the database

Feature lists don't decide this comparison. Staffing does.

**Pick LGL if one person owns the database** and that person likes structure. A dedicated admin, an operations-minded ED, or a long-tenured office manager will get more capability per dollar from LGL than from anything else on the market.

**Pick Bloomerang if data entry is shared** across a development director, an ED, and part-time staff who touch the system twice a week. Software that people avoid opening produces stale data, and stale data is more expensive than any subscription. Bloomerang's premium over LGL is roughly the cost of the database actually getting used.

## The growth question

Both platforms scale by record count, so neither punishes growth with per-seat fees. LGL's ceiling is comfort with its interface as the team grows and less-technical staff join. Bloomerang's ceiling is advanced automation, which most shops under $1M won't hit before they'd outgrow either tool for other reasons.

## How to choose in one week

Take advantage of the fact that both vendors make trying easy. LGL offers a free trial with no contract waiting behind it. Bloomerang's team runs a useful demo; book one, bring three real donor records, and ask to see the retention dashboard populated with sample data. Read [our Bloomerang review](/platforms/bloomerang/) for the full pricing breakdown before the call.

Then ask the one question that settles it: who, by name, will open this system every day? Buy for that person.
