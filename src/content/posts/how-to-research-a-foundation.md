Vetting a foundation before you apply takes about 20 minutes and saves you a week of wasted writing. Check four things in order: do they accept unsolicited proposals, do they fund your issue and geography, what do they actually give (the 990, not the website), and can you find a warm connection. If a funder fails an early check, stop — most should.

## The 20-minute check, in order

Run these in sequence and bail the moment a funder fails. The order matters: the cheapest disqualifiers come first.

**1. Do they accept unsolicited proposals?** Many foundations fund only invited or pre-existing grantees. The website or the 990 usually says so. This is the fastest disqualifier and the most commonly ignored one — no amount of proposal quality overcomes "by invitation only." If it's invitation-only, the funder isn't dead to you; it just moves to the relationship track instead of the proposal track.

**2. Do they fund your issue and your geography?** Most foundations restrict giving to specific fields and regions. A tool like [Instrumentl](/go/instrumentl) filters both at once; [Grants.gov](/platforms/grantsgov/) covers the federal side. Rule out anyone outside your lane before you read a word of program language — geography kills more applications than quality does.

**3. What do they actually fund?** Now open the 990 — free on ProPublica's Nonprofit Explorer, or in depth through [Candid's Foundation Directory](/platforms/candid/) (free on-site at hundreds of partner libraries). Three things to check against your ask:

- **Grant sizes.** If their checks cluster at $10–25k and you need $100k, that's a cultivation-stage funder, not a lead-gift prospect.
- **Grantee types.** Organizations like yours, or hospitals and universities with familiar names? Some funders' "education" giving is entirely to institutions with endowments.
- **Recency and pattern.** Three years of filings, minimum. One year proves nothing; a three-year pattern is a real priority. And when the giving pattern contradicts the website's stated priorities, [believe the 990](/blog/funder-priorities-vs-990/).

**4. Can you find a connection?** A board member, a shared funder, a peer organization they already support. Warm beats cold by a wide margin in foundation giving — many funders behave as relationship-first even when they nominally accept applications. Check your board list against their trustee list before every approach; it's two minutes and occasionally it's gold.

## Turn the check into a scorecard

The value is the discipline: 20 minutes to decide whether to spend a week. Keep it brutally simple — four columns: accepts proposals (y/n), fits issue and geography (y/n), giving range matches (y/n), connection (y/n). Only funders passing all four earn a proposal. Funders passing three get logged with a note on what's missing. Everyone else gets dropped without guilt.

Done consistently, this is the difference between a grant program that chases and one that wins — not better writing, just refusing to write for funders whose own filings said no.
