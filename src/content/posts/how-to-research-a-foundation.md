Vetting a foundation before you apply takes about 20 minutes and saves you a week of wasted writing. Check four things in order: do they accept unsolicited proposals, do they fund your issue and geography, what do they actually give (the 990, not the website), and can you find a warm connection. If a funder fails an early check, stop — most should.

## The 20-minute check, in order

Run these in sequence and bail the moment a funder fails. The order matters: the cheapest disqualifiers come first.

**1. Do they accept unsolicited proposals?** Many foundations fund only invited or pre-existing grantees. Their website or 990 usually says so. This is the fastest disqualifier and the most common one people ignore. If it's invitation-only, no amount of proposal quality helps — you need a relationship first.

**2. Do they fund your issue and your geography?** Most foundations restrict giving to specific fields and regions. A tool like [Instrumentl](/go/instrumentl) filters funders by both at once; [Grants.gov](/platforms/grantsgov/) covers the federal side. Rule out anyone outside your lane before you read a word of their program language.

**3. What do they actually fund?** Now open the 990. Look at grant sizes (is your ask in their range?), grantee types (organizations like yours?), and recency. A foundation that funds your field but only cuts $50k checks to hospitals is not your funder, whatever the website says.

**4. Can you find a connection?** A board member, a shared funder, an alum. Warm beats cold by a wide margin in foundation giving. Check your network — and if you work in a specific sector, your existing relationships are an advantage most applicants don't have.

## Turn the check into a habit

The value is in the discipline: 20 minutes to decide whether to spend a week. Keep a simple scorecard — accepts proposals (y/n), fits issue/geo (y/n), giving range matches (y/n), connection (y/n) — and only funders that pass all four earn a proposal. Everyone else gets logged and dropped. Done consistently, this is the difference between a grant program that chases and one that wins.
